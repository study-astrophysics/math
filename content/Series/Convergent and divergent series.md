

If a series has a finite sum, it is called *convergent*. Otherwise it is called *divergent*.

---
## Partial sum

It is the sum of the first $n$ terms of the series:

$$ S_n = a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + \cdots + a_n. $$


$$ \lim_{n\to\infty} S_n = S.$$


1. If the partial sums $S_n$ of an infinite series tend to a limit $S$, the series is called *convergent*. Otherwise it is called *divergent*.
2. The limiting value $S$ is called the *sum of the series*.
3. The difference $R_n = S - S_n$ is called the *remainder* (or the remainder after $n$ terms). We see that

$$ \lim_{n\to\infty} R_n = \lim_{n\to\infty}(S-S_n) = S - S = 0. $$
